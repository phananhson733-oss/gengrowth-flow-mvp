## A Full Natal Chart vs a Sun-Sign Label: What Actually Differs

### How a Sun-sign label works

A Sun-sign label compresses everything into one archetype. It is fast and portable — "Cancer: emotional, nurturing" in three seconds — which is why it dominates casual talk. The cost is resolution: it hides the Capricorn Moon's discipline and the Leo Venus–Mars flair that make his public read feel controlled rather than purely sentimental. Call him "a typical emotional Cancer" and the phrase slides off a player known for measured, unhurried decisions.

### How a full chart works

A full natal chart stacks the placements so the contradictions stay visible at once — the protective Cancer core, the disciplined Capricorn tempo, the expressive Leo drive. Following the depth-psychology reading associated with Liz Greene, it trades simplicity for nuance. The one caveat is that it can only go as far as the data allows, and here the house and rising layers are simply missing, because no birth time is on record.

### Which one to reach for

The trade-off is honest. A label hands you a headline you can trust at a glance; the chart hands you a portrait you have to sit with. For a persona lens — what most fans are actually after — the layered version wins, as long as you treat the missing Ascendant as unknown instead of filling it in.
