# Outrank Alternatives — Work Out Which Half You Are Replacing First

## What Is Outrank, and What Are You Actually Replacing?

**Outrank is a content automation platform that bundles three separate jobs — keyword research, article generation, and scheduled publishing to your CMS — behind one subscription.** Most people searching for an alternative only want one of the three replaced.

- **The published price is $99/month.** Its pricing page listed an All-in-One plan at $99/month or $999/year, covering 30 auto-published articles per month, automated keyword research, backlink exchange access, AI images and unlimited users (checked 17 August 2026).
- **Volume is an add-on, not a tier.** 60 articles/month adds $85, 90 adds $160. Human editing is a separate $1,399/month service.
- **The output is the product.** Thirty articles a month published on a schedule is the thing you are buying — not a research tool that happens to draft.
- **So "alternative" is ambiguous.** Replacing the research is a different purchase from replacing the writing, and both are different from replacing the publishing pipeline.

Work out which of the three you actually want before comparing anything, because the tools below are not priced on the same unit — and the pillar comparison in [[<TBD-internal-link: our guide to cheap SEO tools>]] covers the budget end of that split.

## Why It Matters Which Half You Are Replacing

Per-article cost is the number every roundup of Outrank alternatives leads with, and it is close to meaningless across these products because they do not meter the same thing.

Outrank meters **articles**: 30 a month at $99 works out to $3.30 each. Arvow meters **credits** — its pricing page lists Solo at $39/month for 1,000 credits with articles costing roughly 10 credits each, so about 100 articles, except $39 is a promotional rate against a $99 regular price (checked 17 August 2026). RightBlogger meters **SEO reports**: Solo at $59/month covers 30 reports, which is not an article count at all.

Three products, three units. A table that prints $3.30, $0.39 and $59 side by side is comparing an article to a credit to a report, and the cheapest-looking row is usually the one whose unit does the least.

## How Outrank Alternatives Actually Differ in Real Agency Workflows

The useful split among Outrank alternatives is not by price tier. It is by which of the three jobs the tool refuses to do.

### Tools That Replace the Whole Pipeline

Arvow and Byword sit closest to Outrank: research, draft and publish in one flow. Arvow's published tiers run Solo $39, Business $69 and Ultimate $249 per month against regular prices of $99, $149 and $449, with credits resetting monthly and no rollover. Byword's pricing page is headed "Plans Starting at $99/month"; we could not read its tier detail directly, so treat anything below that headline as unverified.

These are like-for-like swaps. If Outrank's output volume is what you want and only the price or the writing quality bothers you, this is the row to shop.

### Tools That Replace Only Part of It

RightBlogger prices by site and by report rather than by article: Solo $59/month for one site and 30 SEO reports, Pro $89 for three sites and 90 reports, Agency $299 for ten or more sites and 300 reports. It carries a 7-day free trial and a 30-day money-back guarantee (checked 17 August 2026).

That is a different purchase. You are buying recurring site-level analysis, not a publishing schedule, and comparing its monthly price against Outrank's tells you nothing about whether either fits.

## Common Misreadings When Comparing These Tools

Four patterns cost people money when they shortlist Outrank alternatives:

1. **Reading a promotional price as the price.** Arvow's $39 Solo tier sits against a $99 regular rate, and its yearly discount is time-limited. Budget against the regular number unless you are committing annually today.
2. **Treating credits as articles.** Credits are also spent on images — roughly 3 per AI image on Arvow — and they do not roll over. A 1,000-credit month is not 100 articles if you also want art.
3. **Assuming a bundled backlink feature is free of risk.** Both Outrank and RightBlogger advertise a backlink exchange. Google's spam policies name ["excessive link exchanges ('Link to me and I'll link to you') or partner pages exclusively for the sake of cross-linking"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) as link spam, and say sites that violate the policies "may rank lower in results or not appear in results at all." The feature existing in the product does not make the practice safe; how you use it is on you.
4. **Ignoring the volume question entirely.** Google's same spam policies define scaled content abuse as generating many pages "for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users," and name using AI tools to generate many pages without adding value as an example. Thirty to ninety posts a month is inside the range where that judgement gets made about your site, whichever vendor produced them.

## How to Evaluate an Outrank Alternative

Three questions separate real Outrank alternatives from a lateral move. Work through them in this order:

1. **Which of the three jobs is failing?** If the drafts are the problem, a cheaper pipeline tool with the same writing model changes nothing. If the publishing integration is the problem, that is a much narrower search.
2. **What does one month actually cost at your volume?** Convert every candidate to your own unit — articles you will genuinely publish — including images, and using regular rather than promotional pricing.
3. **Who edits before publish?** Every vendor in this category, Outrank included, is a drafting system. Outrank sells human editing separately at $1,399/month, which is a fair signal of what that step costs when you do not do it yourself.

If none of the three has a clear answer, the honest conclusion is that a tool swap is not your bottleneck.

## How to Run a Two-Week Trial Step by Step

1. **Pick one real content brief you already planned to publish** — not a test topic. Vendor demos look identical on generic subjects.
2. **Run it through two candidates**, and count the editing minutes each draft needs before you would publish it. That number, not the subscription price, is the real cost difference.
3. **Check the publishing integration against your actual CMS**, including images, internal links and canonical handling.
4. **Turn any backlink-exchange feature off** for the trial, so you are evaluating the content on its own.
5. **Audit what you published** after two weeks — thin pages, duplicate angles, orphaned posts. [Run the free SEO audit](https://gengrowth.ai/tools/seo-audit) against the site and read the result before renewing anything.

Step five is the one people skip, and it is the one that tells you whether the volume helped. Internal linking is usually where automated output falls down first, which [[<TBD-internal-link: how internal link structure moves authority>]] explains in more detail.

## Common Questions About Outrank Alternatives

**What is the cheapest Outrank alternative?**

On published entry prices, Arvow's Solo tier at $39/month promotional ($99 regular) is the lowest in this set, but it meters credits rather than articles, so the comparison only holds once you convert to your own volume.

**Is there a free Outrank alternative?**

Not for the full research-write-publish pipeline. Free tiers in this category cover analysis rather than generation, which is a different job.

**Do these tools actually publish to WordPress automatically?**

Outrank advertises native publishing integrations as part of its plan. Confirm your specific CMS and check how each tool handles images and internal links before relying on it.

**Is 30 AI articles a month safe for SEO?**

Google's spam policies do not set a number; they judge whether pages are made primarily to manipulate rankings rather than help users. Volume raises the stakes of that judgement rather than triggering a threshold.

**Should I use a backlink exchange?**

Google names excessive link exchanges as link spam. A vendor bundling the feature does not change the policy, and the risk lands on your domain rather than theirs.

**Is Outrank worth $99 a month?**

If you will genuinely edit and publish 30 drafts a month, that is $3.30 per draft, which is below most freelance rates. If you publish four, you are paying about $25 each.

**What replaces only Outrank's keyword research?**

That is the half our own tools cover, along with several dedicated research platforms. It is also the half where a wrong answer is cheapest to correct.
