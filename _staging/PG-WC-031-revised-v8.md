The friction shows up in a few recurring ways:

1. **The loyalty-versus-love framing.** One side reads the exit as abandoning the team; the other reads staying as abandoning his family. It is a clean Gemini paradox — two true things held at once, with no tidy way to collapse them into a single verdict.
2. **The "just an umbilical cord" jab.** A quip attributed to a French L'Équipe presenter — that he left only to cut a cord — reportedly went viral over the weekend. It reduced a values choice to a logistical errand, exactly the kind of flattening that makes fans argue about the wrong thing.
3. **The hidden question underneath.** Fans aren't really debating scheduling; they're asking whether immediate, lived presence can ever outrank narrative glory — and most people genuinely don't know which they'd pick.

A choice like this rarely reflects indecision. More often it reflects a chart whose core value system was never actually in conflict, even when everyone watching assumed it had to be.

## Common Misreadings

Popular write-ups tend to flatten his chart into a one-line verdict, and the same few misreadings keep coming up:

1. **"Gemini means flaky."** The actual pattern is prioritising immediate connection over abstract obligation — not an inability to commit, but a different ranking of what counts as commitment in the first place.
2. **"The anaretic Mars is just a strength."** A 29th-degree Mars is double-edged: tradition reads it as crisis, karmic completion, and urgency, not only peak intensity. It can show up as decisiveness under pressure *and* as a tendency to force a moment before its time.
3. **"Leo Rising makes it all about ego."** Even if the Rising is real, the visible confidence is the entrance, not the motive; the decision to leave reads as Sun-led presence, not showmanship.
4. **"The chart predicts how he'll perform."** A birth chart describes tendencies and patterns, not fixed outcomes — a lens on how he chooses, never a forecast of results.
