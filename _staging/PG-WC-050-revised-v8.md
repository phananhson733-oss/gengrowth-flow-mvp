## Symbolic Match Framing vs a Scoreline Forecast: What Actually Differs

A scoreline forecast weighs form, injuries, and market odds to name a probable result; its whole value is the number at the end. Symbolic framing does a different job: it reads each chart for theme and tension, treats the tournament sky as a shared mood, and describes leanings rather than a winner. The two get confused because they discuss the same match — but blending them is the mistake. The moment a symbolic read says "the stars say France wins," it becomes a deterministic claim it cannot support and loses its integrity.

So this piece keeps its lane: it names a symbolic edge and the match scenario that activates it, then stops short of the final whistle. This is the one section where that caveat needs stating — everywhere else, we get specific about France, Spain, and 2026.
